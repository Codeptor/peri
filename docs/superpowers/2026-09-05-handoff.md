## 2. Why peri is stopped

The operator stopped it on 09-03 to trade the account by hand, and it stayed
stopped. It is **not** paused — `paused=0` — so `systemctl --user start
peri.service` resumes full autonomous trading immediately.

To bring it back for **feed only** (news, Telegram, Trench bias, calendar) with
no ability to open a position:

```bash
ssh remote-ubuntu 'cd ~/botta && uv run python -c "
from peri.state import State; State(\"peri.db\").set_paused(True, \"operator: handoff\")"'
ssh remote-ubuntu 'systemctl --user start peri.service'
```

Pause is gate 2 in `gate_open` — it blocks new entries only. **The analyst can
still close positions.** That surprised the operator on 08-31 when it closed a
manual PUMP trade; if positions must be untouchable, stop the service instead.

While peri is stopped the **news/Telegram/bias feed is dead** — the ingest lives
in the daemon. On 09-04 the newest news item was 25 hours old.

### Highlights worth knowing

**Vision (`src/peri/vision.py`).** `Feed.ingest` opened with `if not text:
return False`, so a caller posting a chart with no caption produced no ledger
row, no wake, nothing. Images are now transcribed once at ingest by the
analyst's own model (`qwen3.8-max` is multimodal — no second vendor), stored in
`tg_messages.image_desc`, rendered as an `IMAGE:` line. Budgeted 30/hour, 8 MB,
own 2-thread pool. Verified against real `media/caller1_*.jpg` — it pulls SOL-USD
5m off french.ag with funding, OI and the caller's TP/SL zones.

**Shutdown.** Took three attempts, each failure instructive:
1. Released an `RLock` from the event loop after acquiring it in a worker — a
   test caught it; it would have crashed every shutdown.
2. Waited on `_trade_lock`, which spans the whole cycle including the LLM call —
   timed out at 75 s and earned a SIGKILL.
3. Even with the right lock it took 100 s to die: `asyncio.run` joins the default
   executor and `to_thread` calls cannot be interrupted.

Final shape: catch SIGTERM → wait on `_execution_lock` (the venue-call + ledger
-write window only, 30 s) → flush → `os._exit(0)`. Measured **0 s, exit 0**.

**Analyst latency.** `max_tokens` is **not** applied to reasoning on the
DashScope endpoint — measured: the same prompt at `max_tokens=3000` emitted
8,176 completion tokens and ran **slower** (225.8 s) than at 9000 (178.6 s). The
retry lever is the **model**: `deepseek-v4-flash-0731` answered the identical
prompt in 80.7 s vs qwen's 178.6 s. Retries now swap model
(`ANALYST_FALLBACK_MODEL`) and keep the budget. A total deadline
(`total_deadline_secs = 420`) bounds the whole decision; the old bound was
`retries × timeout_secs` = 900 s, which a caller wake actually hit.

## 4. Open items

**`_synthesize_orphan_close` drops the entry fee.** A round trip with no ledger
row gets reconstructed so PnL and cooldowns are not lost, but without the
opening fee. Observed 09-02: SOL recorded **+$3.4788** where the true net was
**+$3.19** (entry fee $0.292 omitted), and filed as `source="own"` rather than
`manual`. This inflates the measured record the analyst learns from. Fix in
`Engine._synthesize_orphan_close` (`src/peri/engine.py`).

**`FEE_RATE` overstates builder-dex fees.** Flat 7.5 bp/side in code. Measured:
native taker 7.50 bp, but `xyz` fills came in at **3.30–3.90 bp**. `userFees`
reports `userCrossRate 0.00045` / `userAddRate 0.00015`. The projected-net-TP
gate therefore refuses xyz setups that would clear. Worth a per-dex rate.

**Trailing stop is untested live.** `trail_start_r = 0.5`, `trail_atr_mult =
1.0`, unit-tested (8 tests) but no position has trailed in production yet.
Note the constraint discovered on 08-31: a trail wide enough to clear the noise
band cannot capture a move **smaller than ~1× ATR**. On CL the peak was 1.8×
ATR (trailing would have worked); on the SOL scalp it was 0.66× (it could not).

**Unverified live:** telethon `download_media(file=bytes)` on a real group photo;

## 5. Operational notes

### Scripts (root)

`manual_trade.py` and `manage_trade.py` **both referenced the pre-multi-dex
`cfg.universe.dex`** and would have crashed before sending. Fixed this session
(uncommitted — see §7). `manual_trade.py` also carried the old 10.5 bp fee model,
corrected to 7.5.

- `manual_trade.py MARKET side --notional --stop --tp --leverage --why [--yes]`
  — one market order with brackets, no gates.
- `manage_trade.py MARKET --stop X --tp PRICE:SIZE [--yes]` — replace protection;
  new triggers placed **before** old ones are cancelled.
- `rest_entry.py MARKET side --entry --stop --tp --notional --leverage [--yes]`
  — parks a **maker limit** with SL+TP in one signed action; refuses a limit on
  the wrong side of the mark, or a stop inside the liquidation band (and names
  the highest leverage that would work). **Prefer this over `manual_trade.py`.**
- `close_trade.py MARKET [--yes]` — closes against the **venue** position (so it
  works on positions peri never recorded), cancels leftover triggers, and writes
  the ledger close.

Full instructions for trading this account by hand, written to be read cold:
**`docs/superpowers/manual-trading-runbook.md`**.

### Gotchas

- Remote `python3` has no project deps — use `$HOME/.local/bin/uv run python`.
- `timeout N uv ...` fails with `No such file or directory` on a
  non-interactive shell; use the full uv path.
- `frontendOpenOrders` needs a `dex` param per builder dex. `userFills` does
  **not** — it returns every dex regardless.
- `clearinghouseState` and `frontendOpenOrders` are **not atomic**: seconds after
  a fill the order has left the book while the position has not yet appeared.
  Check `userFills` before concluding an order was cancelled.
- `sz=0.0` with `isPositionTpsl: true` is a **position-level** trigger covering
  the whole position, not a zero-size order.

## 6. What the trading actually taught

The account went **$59.65 → $34.14** over the period. The pattern is consistent
enough to state as a rule.

**Entry discipline dominated every other factor.**

| trade | entry | net |
|---|---|---|
| SOL mirror, **chased** to 102.58 (caller said 102.37) | RR 2.11 → **1.05** | **+$0.67** |
| SOL mirror, **rested** at his 103.12 | RR 1.50 held | **+$3.19** |
| BTC swing, rested at a shelf | RR 2.15 | **+$4.98** |
| ETH short, rested at the 24h high | RR 5.67 | **+$5.33** |
| BTC long, **market-bought** at 0.96 of range | RR 1.82 | **−$9.92** |
| BTC long, **market-bought** at the spike top | — | **−$13.06** |

Every winner waited for a level. Every large loser bought or sold at market into
a local extreme. Direction was rarely the problem.

**Size, not direction, caused the drawdown.** The final BTC long was $1,680 of
notional on a $47 account — 36× leverage on equity. One correctly-placed stop
cost **31% of the account**. To recover $34 → $59 needs **+75%**.

**Fees set a floor on what is worth trading.** Round trip is ~0.15% of notional.
On a 0.5% scalp target that is a third of the move; on a 4% swing it is
negligible. The 40x scalp made **+$1.66 gross → +$0.30 net**. A short-then-long
flip on $1,680 cost **$2.39 in fees alone**.

**Stops are the whole game.** The two unprotected positions round-tripped +$4.32
→ −$9.92 and +$2.02 → −$13.06 with no mechanism to keep anything. The stopped
position lost a *known* $13.06 instead of an open-ended amount — BTC fell
another 834 points after the stop fired, and liquidation would have hit.
